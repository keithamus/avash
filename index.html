About

      
    

    

      A BlurHash/
      ThumbHash replacement,
      which uses a single 48 px AV1 frame with the container headers stripped.
      Include the avash.js library on your page (1kb
      gz) and it will look for any images with the `avash` attribute
      (<img avash="..." src="...">) and the content of the
      attribute will be rendered as an AVIF background image, displaying before
      the browser has downloaded the `src` image.
    

    

      To create an avash hash, pick a sample or upload an image and copy the
      hash (it uses rav1e via
      wasm). Tweak the sliders and the hash will change, as will the image
      output, letting you find the right balance for your image.
    

    

      BlurHash images at the smallest number of
      components will be smaller than Avash, but a BlurHash at 5x8
      will likely be less clear than the Avash at the same length (click "Match
      BlurHash length" to find encoding settings for Avash to match BlurHash
      length). Avash has slight quantization artefacts compared to BlurHash, but
      Blurhash smears colours. Compare kodim09 or
      kodim16 to see this smearing effect. BlurHash requires a
      2.5kb library to decode images.
    

    

      ThumbHash is often far
      smaller (typically 28 characters), which is very compact. Avash will
      always be bigger, even at the worst quality. ThumbHash images are
      unrecognisable and so mostly help reduce the visually jarring effect of
      having images pop in, but offer no real hint to the user about
      what the image might be when fully downloaded. Compare `kodim04`
      - an image of a person, to see that a ThumbHash is not recognisably a
      person, while Avash at 160 chars is. ThumbHash requires a
      4.15kb library to decode, meaning around 20 images (at
      default Avash settings, or around 30 at 160 chars) would need to be on a
      page for it to pay for itself compared to Avash.
